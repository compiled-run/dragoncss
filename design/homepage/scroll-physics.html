Fall: the bottom roller starts at rest and accelerates, with position proportional to t² scaled by (1 − friction), until the sheet runs out. There's no deceleration, because gravity keeps pulling.

Hard stop: parchment doesn't stretch, so the roller never passes full length. The jolt becomes an upward rebound: a damped spring (stiffness k, damping ratio ζ) that starts with the roller's impact speed and can only pull the roller up, never below full length.

Spin: while it falls, the roller turns. Its surface speed equals the fall speed, shown by the moving ink bands. It stops dead at the jolt.

Sway: the impact sets the whole sheet swinging from the top roller, as a pendulum whose angle decays by e−t/τ with a period of about 1.4 s.

By hand: smooth acceleration and deceleration, with no bounce and no sway. That's a person controlling it.

## 1. What Chrome 145 does (Blink tag 145.0.7632.6, Skia 2ab8add5), measured

- **The reference raster is exact.** `packages/layout/src/paint-gradient.ts` reproduces Chrome's CPU raster of CSS gradients
  pixel for pixel: linear (deg, rad, grad, turn, negative and over 360, sides, magic corners), radial (circle and ellipse, the
  four extents, explicit px and % sizes, px, % and keyword centres), repeating linear and radial (including SkConicalGradient's
  concentric case, a repeating radial whose first stop is not 0), hard stops, two-position stops, transparent and translucent
  stops, currentcolor, several layers over a colour, background-origin and -clip, no-repeat sizes and positions. Research
  probes compared about 14 million pixels at DPR 2, 3 and 2.625, abspos and flex boxes, whole and fractional: all equal at
  channel delta 0. The committed proof is `bg2-reference.test.ts`: 6 cases, 258 box rasters, 5,340,239 pixels, 0 differing.
- **Blink's tan and atan2 are Apple libm's.** EndPointsFromAngle calls `tan(float)` and the magic corners `atan2(float, float)`;
  libc++ routes both to tanf and atan2f of the capture host, which are not correctly rounded (tanf differs from the correctly
  rounded tan on 6% of all 0.01-degree gradient angles). Both are ported from the arm64 disassembly of libsystem_m (double
  polynomials with fmadd; constants in the source) and equal the host's on 336,000 and 320,000 arguments. The correctly
  rounded tan changes Chrome's pixels (the `tanCorrectlyRounded` plant differs on 2 of 54 probed angle boxes).
- **hypotf** equals `(float)sqrt((double)x*x + (double)y*y)` on the host (20 million samples). The translator subset has no
  sqrt: `sqrtF64` is Newton's method plus exact Dekker midpoint tests (equal to Math.sqrt on 50,000 values), `fma64` a
  Dekker/TwoSum fused multiply-add (equal to BigInt on 20,000).
- **The shader matrix and dither are relative to the cc raster tile** (T109): 512 px tiles on the macOS host at DPR >= 2,
  bitmaps starting at 510 i. The device reproduces the capture host's tiling at the box's page position.
- **Blink draws one tile two ways.** `OptimizeToSingleTileDraw` decides: if one tile holds the dest at its *unsnapped* size
  (DrawTiledBackground), or at its *snapped* size for the bottom layer when its clip is border-box and the tile covers the
  snapped dest (PaintFastBottomLayer), the gradient shader draws on the page's cc tile. Otherwise the tile goes through the
  picture shader (GeneratedImage::DrawPattern): the tile image is rastered in its own space (dither and matrix start at the
  tile) and drawn with an image shader (srcover of 8-bit premultiplied pixels, no dither). Measured on 36 classified boxes and
  then on 1.98 million pixels of fractional abspos and flex boxes with one to three layers: this rule and both models are
  exact. (The first hypothesis, direct draws only, failed on padding-box clips in fractional boxes: T074 research log.)
- **Real tiling is refused.** A layer whose tile does not cover its snapped dest repeats the picture tile (with resampling
  for fractional tiles). It is not modelled: the compiler refuses it and the device flags it (`modelled` false).
- **No-repeat geometry uses the unsnapped positioning area** (percentages of unsnapped available space): a snapped model
  fails 6 of 24 fractional no-repeat cases.
- **Legacy colour alpha is 8-bit** in the gradient stops too: rgba(255, 0, 0, 0.46) is 117/255 (96,000 pixels equal).
- **BorderEdge::ObscuresBackground** hides the background under an opaque solid side (double below 3 device px): the dest of a
  border-box layer stops at the padding box there.

## 2. Rulings (PM under the Decision Rule, recorded)

1. **Dragon rasterises the stack.** The colour and every gradient layer of a box are rasterised into one premultiplied bitmap,
   composited bottom first over transparent exactly as Skia composites them, and drawn one device pixel per pixel in the
   background-layers stage over the native background colour. A pixel is Chrome's whatever the backdrop only where the stack
   is opaque, so a stack that is not opaque wherever a gradient layer paints is refused (BG2c: translucent backgrounds over a
   backdrop Dragon does not know). Opaque means an opaque colour whose clip holds every gradient layer's clip, or an opaque
   gradient layer repeating on both axes whose clip does. An element with opacity below 1 would isolate its backdrop; that
   waits for PNT1.
2. **Repeat.** repeat, no-repeat, repeat-x and repeat-y. space and round are refused (BG2c). A repeating axis must draw one
   tile: size auto (or cover and contain, which size a gradient to its area), position 0, and a clip box inside the origin box
   (a side that obscures the background counts as inside).
3. **Geometry units.** Gradient stops, radii and centres, background-size and background-position take px and percentages;
   em, rem, viewport units and calc() there are refused (BG2c), as are offsets from the right or bottom edge (they compute to a
   calc()), negative stop positions, and content-box origin or clip on a box with padding (the device reads borders only).
4. **Refused with their package:** url() and image-set() images (REPL, BG2b); conic gradients, colour hints, interpolation
   spaces, background-attachment fixed and local (BG2b); gradients on html and body (they paint the canvas) and on inline
   boxes (BG2c). Every refusal is located at the node it refuses.
5. **The allowance is none.** The TS reference equals Chrome at every exactly painted pixel, so `allowances/gradient.ts`
   stays `GATE_CHANNEL_DELTA` (0), and the gradient rule kind compares at delta 0. No gradient row needs a caveat for an
   allowance; iOS rows are caveat only because paint rows are (profile-rows.ts).
6. **Features.** A layer longhand holding one item keeps its natural feature (`background-image:<linear-gradient()>`,
   `background-size:cover`, `background-position-x:<percentage>`); several layers are `<list>`.

  layer longhand two values of its own syntax (it gave every paint longhand two colours).
- `dragon/test/background.test.ts`: the shorthand now sets every layer longhand; the expansion pins say so, and the refusal
  pins keep only values that are still refused (url(), image-set(), conic, hints, em, right offsets, space, round, fixed,
  local, text).

  conic layer): both now compile, so each is retargeted to a value still refused, keeping its intent.
- The background group's `computedExtra` is dropped: the layer longhands are LONGHANDS now, so both renderings capture them;
  the captures are byte-identical to a capture with computedExtra (JSON key order is first insertion).
